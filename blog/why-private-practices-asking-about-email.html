Why So Many Private Practices Are Suddenly Asking Me About Email

Most of the work I do starts with a website or a search visibility problem. That's true of the majority of requests I get, and it hasn't changed. But lately I've been getting a different question. Not about the website. About email.

Sometimes a welcome sequence for new patients. Sometimes follow-up for people who fill out a contact form and then go quiet. One person recently asked for a monthly note to her list that didn't sound like it was written by a hospital communications department. I didn't think that was a random request. The instinct behind it is right.

The math most practices never run

Everyone on your email list did something to get there. They found you, read enough to decide you were worth trusting with their address, and opted in. That's already a different kind of relationship than someone who clicked through from a search.

And most practices just let that list sit.

No algorithm decides who sees an email. No cost per click. You write something, hit send, and it goes directly to someone who already knows who you are. For a practice working hard to bring the right patients through the door, that's a real asset to leave unused.

That's why I built email into the framework I use with every practice, as two of its four stages.

Where email fits in the ACRR system

I work with practices through a four-stage framework: Attract, Convert, Retain, Refer. Email does real work in two of them.

Convert. A lot of people find a practice, get interested, and then drift off. Not because they decided against it. Life got in the way. A short follow-up sequence, two or three emails after someone fills out a form or downloads something, pulls a real percentage of those people back before they forget. They were already leaning toward you. They just needed something at the right moment.

Retain. This is the stage most practices underinvest in. An existing patient you haven't reached in eight months is quietly becoming less connected to you. Everything is fine until it isn't, and by then the drift has already happened. A short, useful email every few weeks keeps that from going too far. It doesn't need to be long. It just needs to remind people why they chose a practice that thinks the way yours does.

To be clear about what this is: marketing email. Newsletters, re-engagement, practice updates. Clinical communication stays in the patient portal. Nothing here touches protected information.

If you've been thinking about this

More practices are asking about email than a year ago. My read is that a lot of them have a list they built and haven't done much with. If that's you, the most useful thing to figure out first usually isn't what to write. It's where the real gaps are in how you're bringing patients in and keeping them, because email almost always turns out to be one piece of something larger.
