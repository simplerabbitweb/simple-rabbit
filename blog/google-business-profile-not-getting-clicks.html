Here’s why your practice’s Google Business Profile isn’t getting clicks

By Leann Frank · April 10, 2026 · 8 min read

You claimed your Google Business Profile. You filled in your hours and your address. Maybe you even uploaded a few photos. And yet, when someone in your area searches for what you do, your profile sits quietly in the results while a competitor gets the call.

This is one of the most common and frustrating situations for local service businesses. Your GBP is technically live, but it’s not doing anything. No calls. No direction requests. No website clicks. Just a listing that exists, collecting digital dust.

The good news: Google Business Profile performance is almost always fixable. But first you need to understand what Google actually uses to decide who shows up and who gets clicked.

How Google decides who appears in the local pack

The “local pack” is the map section that shows three business listings at the top of a Google search for local services. Getting into that pack, and getting clicked once you’re there, depends on three factors Google uses to rank local businesses:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches what the person searched for
  • Distance: how close your business is to the searcher (or the location they specified)
  • Prominence: how well-established and trusted your business appears to Google, based on reviews, links, activity, and profile completeness

You can’t control distance. But relevance and prominence are almost entirely within your control, and most service businesses are leaving both on the table.

Reason 1: Your business category is wrong or too broad

Your primary category is one of the most important signals you send to Google. It tells the algorithm what type of search you should appear for. If your category is off, you’re invisible to the people most likely to hire you.

A common mistake: choosing a broad category like “Consultant” or “Business Service” when a more specific option exists. Google has hundreds of categories. A family law attorney who selects “Lawyer” instead of “Family Law Attorney” is competing against every type of legal practice in the area instead of showing up specifically for family law searches.

What to do: Go into your GBP dashboard and search for the most specific category that accurately describes your primary service. Add secondary categories for any other services you offer. Google allows up to 10 categories; most businesses use only one.

Reason 2: Your profile description isn’t written for search

The business description on your GBP is 750 characters. Most business owners write something generic like “We provide high-quality services to clients in the area.” That tells Google almost nothing.

Your description should include the specific services you offer, the location you serve, and the types of clients you work with. Not in a robotic, keyword-stuffed way. Just clearly and specifically.

For example, instead of: “We offer comprehensive health services for patients of all ages,” write: “Dr. Sarah Kim is a functional medicine physician in Austin, TX, offering cash-pay care for women dealing with hormonal imbalances, fatigue, and thyroid issues whose conventional labs keep coming back normal.”

The second version gives Google clear signals about what you do, where you do it, and who you do it for. That specificity directly affects whether your profile appears for relevant local searches.

Reason 3: You don’t have enough reviews, or they’re too old

Reviews are one of the most powerful ranking factors for local search, and they affect click-through rate even more than ranking position. A business ranked third with 47 five-star reviews will often get more clicks than the business ranked first with 6 reviews from three years ago.

Google looks at:

  • Volume: how many reviews you have total
  • Recency: when your most recent reviews were posted
  • Rating: your average star score
  • Response rate: whether you reply to reviews, both positive and negative

A profile with 4 reviews, the most recent posted 14 months ago, signals to both Google and potential clients that this business isn’t very active. That hurts your ranking and your credibility simultaneously.

What to do: Build a simple review request into your client workflow. After a project wraps up or a service is delivered, send a short personal note asking for a Google review and include your direct review link. Most happy clients will leave one; they just need to be asked. Aim for consistency over quantity: two or three new reviews a month does more for your profile than a burst of ten followed by silence.

Reason 4: Your photos are outdated, low quality, or missing entirely

Google Business Profiles with photos receive significantly more clicks than those without. According to Google’s own data, businesses with photos get 42% more requests for directions and 35% more website clicks than businesses without photos.

Photos do two things: they signal activity (Google rewards profiles that are regularly updated), and they help potential clients decide whether your business looks like the right fit before they even click.

What to upload:

  • A clear, professional photo of you or your team
  • Your workspace, studio, or office if you have one
  • Before and after shots, finished work, or project photos
  • Your logo as the profile image
  • A compelling cover photo that represents your brand

Skip the stock photos. Google can’t tell the difference, but your potential clients can, and it undermines the trust you’re trying to build.

Reason 5: You haven’t filled out your services section

Many service businesses leave the Services section of their GBP empty or barely filled in. This is a significant missed opportunity.

Google uses your services list to match your profile against specific service searches. If someone searches for “functional medicine NP near me” and that type of care doesn’t appear anywhere in your profile, Google has no reason to surface you for that query.

For each service you offer, add:

  • The service name (be specific: “Custom WordPress Website Design” beats “Web Design”)
  • A description of 150–300 characters that naturally includes how you deliver it and who it’s for
  • A price or price range if you’re comfortable sharing it

Think of each service entry as a micro landing page for that specific offering. The more clearly you describe it, the more accurately Google can match you to relevant searches.

Reason 6: You’re not posting to your profile

Google Posts are short updates you can publish directly to your Business Profile, announcing offers, sharing articles, highlighting services, or posting updates. Most businesses set up their profile once and never post again.

Posting regularly does two things. First, it signals to Google that your business is active, which is a soft ranking factor. Second, your posts appear on your profile in search results, giving potential clients more to engage with before they decide to click.

You don’t need to post every day. Once or twice a week is enough to keep your profile fresh. Share a recent client win (with permission), link to a new blog post, highlight a service, or answer a common question. Each post is another surface area for someone to find and connect with your business.

Reason 7: Your website is hurting your GBP, not helping it

This one surprises people. Your Google Business Profile and your website are not independent. Google uses your website to validate and enrich your GBP. It looks at your site to confirm your location, services, and expertise before deciding how prominently to rank your profile.

If your website is slow, poorly structured, or doesn’t clearly mention the services and locations on your GBP, it creates a disconnect Google notices. A well-built website with proper local SEO, including location-specific pages, clear service descriptions, and consistent NAP (name, address, phone number). This reinforces your GBP and improves your local ranking.

Your GBP and your website work together. Optimizing one without the other is like trying to win a race with one shoe.

This is a big part of what we look at when we work with practices, not just what’s wrong with your site, but how your site and your Google presence are (or aren’t) working together to get you found and clicked.

Reason 8: Your NAP information is inconsistent across the web

NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone Number. Google cross-references your business information across dozens of online directories, including Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, industry directories, and local chamber listings, and uses consistency as a trust signal.

If your business name is listed as “Simple Rabbit LLC” on your GBP but “Simple Rabbit” on Yelp and “SimpleRabbit Studio” on a local directory, Google sees inconsistency. It doesn’t know which version is correct, and that uncertainty erodes the trust signals that drive local rankings.

What to do: Pick one exact version of your business name, address format, and phone number. Use it everywhere, exactly the same way. Then audit your major directory listings (Google, Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places) and update any that don’t match.

Reason 9: You haven’t verified your profile, or it got unverified

An unverified Google Business Profile has severely limited visibility. Google doesn’t surface unverified profiles with the same confidence as verified ones, and some features (like the ability to respond to reviews or publish posts) may be restricted.

Verification also occasionally lapses. Google sometimes re-requests verification after significant profile changes or if there’s suspicious activity. Log into your GBP dashboard and check that your profile shows as “Verified.” If it doesn’t, complete the verification process immediately.

What to fix first

If your profile isn’t getting clicks, start with the highest-impact changes:

  1. Confirm your primary category is as specific as possible
  2. Rewrite your business description to include your specific services and location
  3. Fill in the Services section completely
  4. Send review requests to your last five happy clients today
  5. Upload at least five current, real photos
  6. Check that your NAP matches across your major listings
  7. Publish your first Google Post

None of these require technical expertise or a big time investment. They do require consistency. A GBP you actively maintain for three months will outperform a GBP you set up perfectly once and ignored.
