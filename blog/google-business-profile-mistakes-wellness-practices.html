Google Business Profile Mistakes That Cost Wellness Practices Real Patients

By Leann Frank · May 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Photo by Vitaly Gariev on Unsplash

Your Google Business Profile is live. You added your hours, uploaded a photo, maybe even wrote a description. And now you're wondering why it's not bringing in new patients.

You're not alone. Most wellness practitioners set up their profile once and forget it. Or they make small mistakes that quietly cost them visibility, clicks, and bookings every single week.

The frustrating part? These mistakes are fixable. Often in an afternoon.

I've reviewed dozens of Google Business Profiles for women's wellness practices. The same problems show up again and again. Here are the ones I see most often, and what to do instead.

Mistake 1: Choosing the Wrong Primary Category

Google gives you one primary category. This single choice affects which searches you show up for more than almost anything else on your profile.

I see practitioners pick broad categories like "Wellness Center" when something more specific exists. Or they choose based on how they think of themselves rather than how patients search.

A pelvic floor physical therapist might select "Physical Therapy Clinic." That's technically accurate. But "Pelvic Floor Physical Therapist" is now a category option. The more specific choice helps you rank for the patients actually looking for your specialty.

What to do: Search Google for the exact service you offer. Look at the profiles that show up in the map pack. Note their primary categories. Then pick the most specific, relevant option available to you. You can add secondary categories too, but the primary one carries the most weight.

Mistake 2: An Incomplete or Generic Description

Your business description is 750 characters of free real estate. Most profiles waste it.

I see descriptions that read like this: "We are a wellness practice dedicated to helping you feel your best. Our team provides quality care in a comfortable environment."

This tells a potential patient nothing. It could describe any practice in any city offering any service.

Your description should answer three questions quickly: What specific services do you offer? Who do you help? Where are you located?

What to do: Rewrite your description with specific details. Name your services. Name the neighborhoods or towns you serve. Mention what makes your practice different. Use natural language, not keyword stuffing. Write for the person reading, not just the algorithm.

Mistake 3: No Photos, or the Wrong Photos

Profiles with photos get more clicks. Google has said this directly. Yet many wellness practices have one blurry exterior shot, or worse, no photos at all.

The photos that matter most: your office interior, your treatment rooms, and you. Patients want to see where they'll be and who they'll meet. A warm, professional space photo does more than any stock image ever could.

What to do: Add at least 5 to 10 photos. Include your waiting area, treatment space, and a professional headshot. Use good lighting. Keep them current. Update seasonally or whenever you make changes to your space. Google favors profiles that show recent activity.

Mistake 4: Ignoring Reviews (or Handling Them Poorly)

Reviews affect your ranking. They also affect whether someone clicks through to your website or calls your office.

Some practitioners feel awkward asking for reviews. Others get reviews but never respond to them. Both hurt you.

Not responding to reviews, even positive ones, signals you're not engaged. Responding poorly to negative reviews can scare off potential patients faster than the negative review itself.

What to do: Create a simple system for requesting reviews from satisfied patients. Respond to every review within a few days. For positive reviews, thank them specifically. For negative reviews, stay calm and professional. Acknowledge their experience, offer to discuss offline, and avoid getting defensive. Future patients are watching how you handle criticism.

Mistake 5: Inconsistent NAP Information

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone number. Google checks whether your NAP is consistent across the internet. When it's not, your credibility drops.

This happens easily. You moved offices two years ago. Your old address still shows on a directory you forgot about. Or you use "Suite 100" on your website but "Ste 100" on your profile.

These small inconsistencies add up. Google gets confused about which information is correct. That confusion costs you visibility.

What to do: Search your practice name and look at every listing that appears. Check directories like Healthgrades, Yelp, Psychology Today, and any specialty directories for your field. Make sure every listing shows the exact same name, address, and phone number. Format them identically.

Mistake 6: No Services Listed

Google lets you add specific services to your profile. Many practitioners skip this entirely.

Services help Google understand what you offer. They also help patients scanning your profile quickly see if you're a fit. Someone searching for "lymphatic drainage massage Bergen County" is more likely to find you if that service is explicitly listed on your profile.

What to do: Add every service you offer. Include a brief description and price if you're comfortable sharing it. This takes fifteen minutes and makes your profile more useful to both Google and potential patients.

Mistake 7: Setting It and Forgetting It

Your profile isn't a one-time task. It's an ongoing asset that rewards attention.

Google notices when profiles are active. Posting updates, adding new photos, responding to reviews, answering questions: all of these signal that your business is alive and engaged.

Profiles that sit untouched for months tend to slip in visibility. Google has no reason to think they're more relevant than competitors who are actively maintaining theirs.

What to do: Set a calendar reminder to update your profile monthly. Post about a service you offer, a seasonal health tip, or an update about your practice. Add a new photo. Check for and answer any questions. Ten minutes a month keeps your profile working for you.

Mistake 8: No Link to Your Website (or the Wrong Link)

Your profile should drive people to your website. But I see profiles that link to a generic homepage when a specific services page or booking page would work harder.

If someone finds you searching "acupuncture for fertility Bergen County," linking them to your fertility acupuncture page makes more sense than dropping them on your homepage to figure out the rest.

What to do: Think about what most people searching for you actually want. Link to the page that answers their question fastest. For many practices, that's a services page or a contact page with booking options. Test different links and watch your analytics to see what converts.

Your Google Business Profile is often the first impression a potential patient has of your practice. Before they see your website, before they read your about page, they see your profile in search results.

A profile with the wrong category, no photos, and zero reviews tells a story. So does a profile that's complete, current, and clearly maintained by someone who cares about their practice.

Fix these mistakes and you give yourself a real advantage over the other practices in your area who haven't bothered.
