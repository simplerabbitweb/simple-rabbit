## The one big finding

You are aiming at the **right topics** but often in the **wrong weight class.**

- **Local SEO / Google Business Profile** (where most of your posts live): the right topics, but those Google results are packed with established SEO agencies (Zocdoc, RepuGen, Coalition, dozens more). A young, low-authority site rarely cracks these head-on.
- **"Private pay," "leaving insurance," "how to market a [X] practice"**: these results are owned almost entirely by **therapy and mental-health** content. **Nobody owns the functional-medicine / women's-wellness version.** This is open territory that is *exactly* your buyer.

**Your winning move is specificity.** Not "local SEO tips" (you'll lose to agencies). Instead: "local SEO for a menopause practice," "leaving insurance as a functional medicine doctor," "website for a private-pay women's health practice." Big agencies won't write these. You can own them.

## Priority order (do these in this sequence)

1. **Claim the open gaps** (Cluster B & C below) — new posts where therapy content ranks and functional-medicine content doesn't exist yet.

3. **Fix what you already have** (cannibalization + retargeting, see "Housekeeping").
4. **Keep local-SEO/GBP posts, but niche them harder** (Cluster D) — stop competing generically.

## Housekeeping (fix these — free wins)

- **Keyword cannibalization:** You have two nearly identical local-SEO posts competing with each other for the same query:
  - `local-seo-for-private-pay-practices.html` — "Local SEO for Private-Pay Practices: 5 Things That Actually Move the Needle"
  - `local-seo-for-private-practices.html` — "Local SEO for Private Practices: The Step-by-Step Playbook"
  Pick the stronger one as the primary, differentiate or redirect the other. Two half-posts split your authority; one strong post ranks better.

- **Opinion posts have a job — just not SEO.** Posts like "Your About Page Is Costing You Patients," "Why Patients Push Back on Your Rates," "The Referral Ceiling" have near-zero search demand. Keep them — they're **excellent for LinkedIn and email** (which you already do). Just don't expect Google traffic from them, and don't count them as SEO.

- **Case studies:** great for *converting* visitors, invisible to search ("Milli Health & Wellness" gets zero searches). Keep them for proof, not traffic.

## The honest caveat

Keyword targeting is half the equation. The other half is **domain authority** — Google trusting your site enough to rank it. Even perfect targeting won't crack saturated SERPs without some authority, which comes from **backlinks** (podcast guest spots, guest posts, directories, partnerships — all of which also put you in front of audiences *now*).
