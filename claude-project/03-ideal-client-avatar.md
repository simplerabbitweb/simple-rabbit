# Simple Rabbit — Ideal Client Avatar

## The Primary Buyer: Dr. Elena Ramirez (Composite Profile)

**Name (composite):** Dr. Elena Ramirez  
**Age:** 40–58  
**Gender:** Female (the buyer is almost always female-coded, even when she's not the only provider)  
**Title:** MD, DO, NP, or PA  
**Specialty:** Functional medicine, integrative medicine, precision medicine, women's health, hormone therapy, or concierge medicine  
**Practice type:** Solo practitioner or very small practice (1–3 providers)  
**Revenue:** $650K–$2M+ private-pay annually  
**Location:** Suburban or mid-size metro, United States  
**Practice model:** Direct-pay only — no insurance billing, no in-network contracts

---

## Her Professional Story

Elena spent 10–18 years in conventional medicine. She burned out. The system rewarded volume, not outcomes. She was seeing 25 patients a day and couldn't do the work she was trained to do. At some point she made the decision — or was pushed into making it — to leave and build something on her own terms.

She's been private-pay for 2–5 years now. The practice is working. Patients love her. The word-of-mouth is solid. But growth is inconsistent, she doesn't fully understand where new patients come from, and the website she launched two years ago feels like it's from a different era. She knows it's costing her, but she doesn't know what "better" looks like — and she doesn't have time to figure it out.

She is not a marketer. She never wanted to be. She went to medical school to practice medicine. The fact that running a private-pay practice requires her to think about SEO, Instagram, and email lists is an annoyance she tolerates, not an identity she's embraced.

---

## Psychographics

**Values:**
- Autonomy above all. She left the insurance system to control her practice. She will not trade one set of constraints for another.
- Clinical depth. She takes the time to do the work right. She expects the same from her vendors.
- Privacy and boundaries. She is not looking for a personal brand influencer situation. She wants a professional practice, not a social media presence.
- Sustainability. She is not trying to go viral or scale to 500 patients. She wants a full, well-paying practice that she can sustain for 15+ years.

**Fears:**
- Looking cheap, generic, or like "every other functional medicine practice"
- Attracting the wrong patient (price-shoppers, non-compliant, chronically dissatisfied)
- Wasting money on marketing that doesn't work
- Being dependent on a vendor who disappears after launch
- Having to become her own IT department
- Her website misrepresenting her work or violating any compliance norms

**Goals:**
- Consistent, qualified new patient flow (not feast-or-famine)
- A website that reflects the quality of her practice
- Freedom from marketing and technical maintenance
- More time — for patients, for herself, for her family
- A practice that doesn't consume her life

## Her Three Stages (which stage she's in changes the messaging)

### Stage A: Considering leaving insurance
Still in-network, 6–18 months away from going private-pay. Quietly researching. No website strategy yet because no private practice yet. What she needs: proof that leaving insurance is doable and that a website is part of how it works.

### Stage B: Recently left insurance (0–18 months private-pay)
Has a basic website (Squarespace, friend's design, or leftover from a group practice). It's not bringing in patients. She's relying on word-of-mouth and feeling exposed. She knows the website is the problem but not what to do about it.

### Stage C: Established but plateaued (2+ years private-pay)
Practice runs. The website was okay when it launched but feels stale. She knows it's costing her but rebuilding feels like a project she doesn't have time for.

**Most valuable buyer:** Stage C. Has revenue, has proof of concept, is ready to invest, and is tired of the status quo.

**Real quote (from a sales call, included verbatim in the messaging map):**
> "I want to work for myself. I don't want to work for anyone anymore. I want to be successful. I want to not have to worry about money. I want to be able to go on vacation or take time with my kids when I need to... I'm 45 years old and I'm not the healthiest person I've been in a long time. The sale is never really about the website. The website is the bridge to the life she's trying to build.

## Her Buying Behavior

- **Researches extensively** before reaching out. Will read the website, the blog, and possibly find Leann on LinkedIn or through a referral before any contact.
- **Trust-driven.** She is not buying on price. She is buying on "does this person understand my world?"
- **Skeptical of marketing.** She can spot generic agency language from a mile away and will bounce if the copy sounds corporate, hype-y, or generic.
- **Wants clarity.** She does not want to schedule a call just to get pricing. She wants to know what the package is, roughly what it costs, and whether it's for her — before she commits 30 minutes to a discovery call.
- **Decisive once convinced.** She is not the type to require 5 follow-ups. If she's convinced, she moves. If she needs 5 follow-ups, she was never the right fit.
- **Referral-friendly.** She talks to other practitioners. If she has a good experience, she tells colleagues. A single satisfied client can generate 3–5 referrals over time.

## Who She Is NOT

Do not write for, market to, or accept as a client:
- Insurance-based practices (different problem set, different budget expectations, different timeline)
- Multi-provider groups with in-house marketing staff (they want agencies, not a one-person studio)
- Practitioners who want to DIY and just need some direction (leads to scope creep and client dissatisfaction)
- Price shoppers who open with "what's the cheapest option" (wrong fit for every offer)
- Practitioners in non-clinical niches (life coaching, business coaching, personal training — different voice, different trust signals)
