The Problem

Why most concierge and DPC websites fail to convert cold patients.

A woman who has never heard of direct primary care lands on your website and has a single question: is this worth it? Not “is this a good doctor”, she can see the credentials. What she wants to know is whether the membership fee makes sense for her life, her health history, and the kind of care she’s been settling for since her OB/GYN stopped taking her insurance. If your site can’t answer that question in thirty seconds, she closes the tab and moves on.

Most concierge and DPC websites don’t even try to answer it. They list the benefits, same-day appointments, longer visits, direct physician access, without explaining why those things matter to a specific patient in a specific situation. The copy reads like a feature list. It doesn’t speak to a woman who is exhausted from eight-minute appointments and a portal that never gets a response.

The second problem is search visibility. A woman who doesn’t know you exists but is ready for something different might search “concierge doctor near me” or “direct primary care for women” or “DPC membership doctor [city].” If your site isn’t showing up for those searches, you’re invisible to the patient who is already motivated and already looking. Local SEO for a DPC or concierge practice requires specific on-page structure, accurate location signals, and a Google Business Profile that matches what your site says.

The membership model is genuinely unfamiliar to most patients. That unfamiliarity is a conversion problem, not a product problem. The right website does the educational work, explaining how DPC works, what the monthly fee covers, and what happens with specialists and labs, so that by the time someone reaches the contact form, she already understands the value and has made up her mind.

The Build

What goes into a website built for a concierge or DPC practice.

The copy comes first. Before any page is designed, I write what it says. For a concierge or DPC practice, that means copy that explains the membership model in plain language, what the fee covers, why it’s structured this way, and what a patient gets that she couldn’t get inside the insurance system. The goal is a patient who arrives informed and ready, not one who needs to be talked into it on the phone.

Design follows the copy. Every site is built from scratch around your practice. No templates, no borrowed structure from a general medical site. The layout, the hierarchy, the visual tone, all of it is built to move a specific kind of patient from curious to ready.

After launch, the site stays current. Concierge and DPC practices evolve, membership tiers change, panels open and close, services expand. Ongoing care means the site reflects where your practice actually is, not where it was eighteen months ago.

Common Questions

Common questions from concierge and DPC practice owners.

How do I explain the membership fee on the website?

The fee needs to be framed as a trade, not a surcharge. The copy should make clear what a patient gets in exchange: longer visits, direct access to her physician, same-day appointments, care that doesn’t get routed through a phone tree. The goal is to answer the “why would I pay this?” question before she has to ask it. Whether or not to display the specific dollar amount is a strategic question we work through in planning, some practices benefit from displaying it clearly, others do better with a “starting at” or an inquiry-based approach. Either way, the framing around the fee matters as much as the number itself.

Should I target women specifically on my concierge or DPC website?

If women are your primary patient population, or the population you want to grow, yes. Women who are tired of having their symptoms dismissed, who are in perimenopause and can’t get a callback, or who manage the healthcare decisions for an entire household are a specific and motivated audience. Copy written to them directly converts better than copy written to a general adult patient. That doesn’t mean you exclude anyone; it means your primary patient feels like the site was written for her, because it was.

How do I rank for “concierge doctor near me”?

Ranking for local searches like that requires a few things to be in place at once: on-page content written around how patients search (not just how the specialty describes itself), accurate and consistent location information across the site, a Google Business Profile that aligns with what the site says, and enough content depth to give search engines something real to index. None of that guarantees a top position, your market and competition play into it too, but sites built with those foundations in place tend to move up over time. For more on how this works for private-pay practices, this article covers the specifics.

What’s different about a DPC site versus a regular practice site?

A standard practice website assumes the visitor already understands how medical care is billed and delivered. A DPC or concierge site can’t make that assumption. The membership model is unfamiliar to most patients, so the site has to do educational work that a conventional practice site doesn’t. That usually means a dedicated “How It Works” page or section, clear answers to the questions every new patient has (What about my insurance? What if I need a specialist? Can I keep my OB/GYN?), and copy that positions the monthly fee as a decision that makes sense rather than a luxury add-on.

Do I need a patient portal integration?

It depends on what your practice management software supports and what you want patients to be able to do from your site. Most concierge and DPC practices use a dedicated EHR that has its own patient-facing portal, Hint Health, Elation, Spruce, or similar, and the website links to it rather than hosting it. We talk through what you’re using in planning and make sure the integration point is clear and easy for a new patient to find. If you want online membership sign-ups handled through the website itself rather than through a third-party tool, that’s a different conversation and worth discussing before the build starts.

Can the site handle membership sign-ups directly?

Yes, with the right setup. Some DPC practices want a fully self-serve flow where a patient can choose a membership tier, enter her information, and set up a payment method without ever talking to the office first. Others prefer a gated application or intake form that lets the practice vet new members before onboarding. Both are buildable. Which approach works better for your practice depends on your current panel capacity, how selective you want to be, and what your existing software supports. That’s part of what we work out in the planning phase before anything is designed.
