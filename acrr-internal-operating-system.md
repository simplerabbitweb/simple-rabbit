# ACRR Patient Growth System · Internal Operating System

Two standalone delivery playbooks, one per package. This is the internal operating system Leann runs the business on. It is not client-facing.

Each playbook is self-contained. To deliver the Strategy package, you only need Playbook A. To deliver the Accelerator, you only need Playbook B. The early phases are intentionally near-identical between the two so refinements copy cleanly from one to the other.

This document assumes solo delivery. Subcontractor handoff is not built in yet.

---

# How to use this document

Each playbook is organized by phase. Each phase has five parts:

1. **Purpose.** What this phase is for, in one or two sentences.
2. **The work.** The actual tasks, in order.
3. **The live call(s).** Structure and agenda for any client calls in the phase.
4. **What good looks like.** The standard each deliverable has to hit before the phase is done.
5. **Decision points and risks.** Where judgment is required and what tends to go wrong.

Before starting any engagement, read the whole relevant playbook once. During the engagement, work phase by phase and do not start a phase until the prior phase passes its "what good looks like" check.

A note that applies to both playbooks: the business stays outside HIPAA. You never access, receive, store, or transmit protected health information. You work only on public-facing marketing assets. You review only anonymized analytics and public pages. If at any point a client tries to give you access to patient data, a patient portal, or anything containing PHI, you stop and redirect them. This is not a preference. It is the boundary the whole business is built on.

# PLAYBOOK A · ACRR Growth Strategy

**The strategic roadmap.** Five to six weeks. $9,500 to $12,500. Your role is expert strategist. You build the plan. The client executes it, with some support from you.

The client is buying clarity and a professional plan. They are not buying implementation. Hold that line throughout. Scope creep on this package usually looks like the client asking you to build the things in the plan. The answer to that is the Accelerator, or a separate retainer.

## Pre-engagement (before Week 1)

**Purpose.** Get the engagement set up cleanly so Week 1 starts on time and on the right foot.

**The work.**
- Send the signed proposal and contract through HoneyBook or 17hats. Confirm the contract includes the language stating you are not a Business Associate and do not handle PHI.
- Collect the deposit. Do not schedule the kickoff call until the deposit clears.
- Send a short welcome email confirming the start date, the kickoff call time, and what the client should expect in Week 1.
- Create the client's project workspace in ClickUp or Notion from the Strategy template.
- Block your own calendar for the phases. Strategy work in Weeks 3 and 4 needs protected deep-work time, not scraps between other things.

**What good looks like.** The client has a confirmed kickoff time, knows what is coming, and has a workspace waiting. You have the next six weeks roughed out on your own calendar.

**Decision points and risks.** If the client is slow to sign or pay, do not start work to be accommodating. The engagement starts when the contract and deposit are in. Starting early to be nice is how timelines slip and scope blurs.

## Phase 1 · Kickoff and Deep Review (Week 1)

**Purpose.** Align on goals, gather everything you need, and form your first read on where the practice is leaking patients.

**The work.**
- Run the kickoff call (see below).
- Immediately after the call, send the intake questionnaire if it was not sent before.
- Review the client's website page by page. Note structure, messaging, clarity, conversion paths, and whether the site communicates the depth of their clinical approach.
- Review the Google Business Profile against the eight-field standard (categories, hours, services, FAQ, photos, reviews and responses, posts, attributes).
- Review the booking process as a patient would. Book a test appointment if possible. Count the clicks. Note the friction.
- Map how the practice currently gets patients. Referrals, word of mouth, search, social, paid. Get the client's own estimate of the mix.
- Review 2 to 3 competitors. Similar local practices in the same niche. Note what they do well and where the client has an opening.

**The live call: Kickoff Call (60 minutes).**
- First 10 minutes: rapport and context. Let the client talk about their practice and how they got here.
- Next 25 minutes: goals. What does success look like in 12 months. What is the patient flow now versus what they want. What have they already tried. What is their ideal patient.
- Next 15 minutes: logistics. Walk through the six weeks, the two upcoming calls, what you need from them and when.
- Final 10 minutes: their questions.
- After the call: send a short recap email with the agreed goals and the questionnaire link.

**What good looks like.** By the end of Week 1 you can state, in plain language, the practice's three biggest patient acquisition problems and you have evidence for each. You understand their goal, their ideal patient, and their current patient mix. The client feels heard and knows what is coming.

**Decision points and risks.** The most common Week 1 failure is a vague goal. "More patients" is not a goal. Push until you have a number and a patient type. If the client cannot define their ideal patient, that is itself a finding and it goes in the plan. Do not move to Phase 2 until the review is genuinely complete. A rushed review produces a generic plan.

## Phase 2 · Dig Deeper (Week 2, async, no live call)

**Purpose.** Turn the Week 1 review into sharp analysis. Define the ideal patient, find the weak spots, and do the research that the plan will be built on.

**The work.**
- Define the ideal patient clearly and in writing. Demographics, clinical profile, what they value, what they search, what makes them a good long-term fit versus a tire-kicker.
- Identify the weak spots in the current system, organized by ACRR pillar. Where is Attract failing. Where is Convert failing. Where is Retain failing. Where is Refer failing.
- Keyword research. Find the actual search terms the ideal patient uses. Separate by intent: symptom searches, specialty searches, credential searches, local searches.
- Note the tools the practice should be using and where the current stack falls short. Recommend HIPAA-compliant options where patient data is involved, so the client stays compliant and you stay clear of it.

**What good looks like.** You have a written ideal-patient definition you would stand behind. You have a weak-spot list organized by ACRR pillar, each item specific and evidence-backed. You have a real keyword list grouped by intent. None of this is generic. All of it is specific to this practice.

**Decision points and risks.** This phase has no call, which means no external deadline pressure. That is the risk. Protect the time. The quality of the plan in Phases 3 and 4 is capped by the quality of the analysis here. Shallow analysis cannot be rescued by good formatting later.

## Phase 3 and 4 · Build the Plan (Weeks 3 to 4, no live call)

**Purpose.** Create the full custom growth plan using the ACRR framework.

**The work.**
- Build the plan pillar by pillar: Attract, Convert, Retain, Refer.
- For Attract: keyword targets, website improvement recommendations, lead magnet ideas, the Google Business Profile plan.
- For Convert: booking process recommendations, lead follow-up recommendations, consultation process improvements.
- For Retain: a new patient welcome plan, patient engagement ideas over time.
- For Refer: simple referral-ask methods, a testimonial collection plan.
- Write the 90-day plan. Specific, sequenced, and realistic for a client executing it themselves with limited time.
- Write the 6 to 12 month plan. The longer arc, less granular, showing where the practice is headed.
- Format the whole thing as a clean, professional document the client can actually use.

**What good looks like.** Every recommendation is specific to this practice, not a generic best practice. The 90-day plan is sequenced so the client knows what to do first, second, third, and it is realistic for someone who is time-poor. A reader could pick up the plan with no other context and know exactly what to do. The document reflects the sophistication of the client's clinical work.

**Decision points and risks.** The two failure modes here are opposite. One is a plan that is too generic, where the recommendations would suit any practice. The fix is to go back to the Phase 2 analysis and tie every recommendation to a specific finding. The other is a plan that is too much, where you have written a 90-day plan that assumes the client has 20 hours a week. They do not. They see 6 to 14 patients a day. The plan has to be executable by a busy physician, or it will sit unused and the engagement will have failed even though the document looked impressive.

## Phase 5 · Present and Fix (Week 5)

**Purpose.** Walk the client through the plan, make sure they understand it and believe in it, and refine based on their feedback.

**The work.**
- Prepare the presentation version of the plan.
- Run the strategy presentation call (see below).
- Capture every piece of feedback during the call.
- Make the agreed changes to the plan after the call.

**The live call: Strategy Presentation Call (90 minutes).**
- First 5 minutes: reframe. Remind them of the goal they set in Week 1, so the plan is heard in that context.
- Next 50 minutes: walk through the plan pillar by pillar. Explain the reasoning, not just the recommendation. The client is analytical and will trust the plan more if they understand why.
- Next 25 minutes: their reactions, questions, and concerns. Capture everything.
- Final 10 minutes: confirm next steps and the Week 6 handover call.

**What good looks like.** The client understands the plan, believes it will work, and can see themselves executing it. They have had every question answered. You have a clear list of the changes they want.

**Decision points and risks.** Distinguish between feedback that improves the plan and feedback that is scope creep. "Can you explain the booking section differently" is refinement. "Can you build the booking system for me" is the Accelerator. When the client wants you to do the implementation, that is a buying signal, not a problem. Name it warmly: this is exactly what the Accelerator is for, and you would be glad to talk about it.

## Phase 6 · Final Handover (Week 6)

**Purpose.** Hand over the finished plan, make sure the client can use it, and open the door to continued work.

**The work.**
- Finalize the clean plan document. Notion or PDF.
- Run the final training and handover call (see below).
- Deliver the final document.
- Send a closeout email with the document, a thank you, and a clear, low-pressure note on the two ways to keep working together (a retainer, or the Accelerator if they decide they want it built for them).

**The live call: Final Training and Handover Call (45 to 60 minutes).**
- First 30 minutes: walk through how to use the strategy document. How to read it, how to sequence the work, how to track progress.
- Next 15 minutes: final questions.
- Final 10 to 15 minutes: next steps. The retainer option, the Accelerator option. Honest and unpushy. If they are not ready, that is fine, and the door stays open.

**What good looks like.** The client has the final document, knows how to use it, and feels equipped to start. The engagement ends cleanly. The client would refer you and would consider working with you again. You know whether there is a next step or not.

**Decision points and risks.** Do not let the engagement trail off. A clean, definite ending with a clear handover is part of the product. The retainer and Accelerator conversation should happen, but it is an offer, not a push. This client buys on trust, and a hard upsell on the last call damages the trust the whole engagement built.

# PLAYBOOK B · ACRR Growth Accelerator

**The full implementation package.** Eleven to twelve weeks maximum. $22,000 to $28,000. Your role is strategist plus builder. You create the plan and you build the entire system for the client. Done-for-you.

The client is buying a finished, working patient growth system and their own time back. They do not want to execute. They want to stay focused on medicine. Hold that frame: your job is to remove the work from their plate, not to assign it back to them.

## Pre-engagement (before Week 1)

**Purpose.** Get the engagement set up cleanly so Week 1 starts on time. This is a longer, higher-value engagement, so the setup matters more.

**The work.**
- Send the signed proposal and contract through HoneyBook or 17hats. Confirm the contract includes the language stating you are not a Business Associate and do not handle PHI.
- Collect the deposit. Do not schedule the kickoff call until the deposit clears.
- Send a welcome email confirming the start date, the kickoff call time, and an overview of the 11 to 12 week arc.
- Create the client's project workspace in ClickUp or Notion from the Accelerator template.
- Block your calendar for the full engagement. The implementation phase has a weekly call every week and protected build time around each. Map all of it now.
- Confirm the tool stack early. The client's CRM and automation platform should be HIPAA-compliant and chosen before the build phase starts, because the build depends on it.

**What good looks like.** The client has a confirmed start, knows the full arc, and has a workspace waiting. Your calendar is blocked for 12 weeks including every weekly call and the build time around it. The tool stack question is at least raised.

**Decision points and risks.** Same as the Strategy package: the engagement starts when the contract and deposit are in, not before. For the Accelerator specifically, the tool stack is a hidden risk. If the client has not chosen a HIPAA-compliant CRM and automation platform by the time the build phase starts, the build stalls. Raise it now, in pre-engagement, not in Week 4.

## Phase 1 · Audit and Strategy (Weeks 1 to 3)

This phase is the same review and planning work as the Strategy package, compressed into three weeks because the plan here leads into a build rather than a handover.

**Purpose.** Build the full ACRR plan that the implementation phase will execute. Get formal approval before any building starts.

**The work.**
- Week 1: run the kickoff call (see below). Send the intake questionnaire. Review the website page by page, the Google Business Profile against the eight-field standard, and the booking process as a patient would experience it. Map how the practice currently gets patients. Review 2 to 3 competitors.
- Week 2: define the ideal patient in writing. Identify the weak spots organized by ACRR pillar. Do the keyword research, grouped by intent. Note the tool stack gaps and recommend HIPAA-compliant options.
- Week 3: build the full custom ACRR plan, pillar by pillar. Then run the strategy presentation and approval call (see below) and get formal sign-off to begin building.

**The live calls.**

*Week 1, Kickoff Call (60 minutes).* Set expectations, understand goals, start building rapport. First 10 minutes rapport and context. Next 25 minutes goals: 12-month success, current versus desired patient flow, what they have tried, ideal patient. Next 15 minutes logistics: walk through the 12 weeks, the weekly call rhythm that starts in Week 4, and what you need from them. Final 10 minutes their questions. Send a recap email after.

*Week 3, Strategy Presentation and Approval Call (90 minutes).* First 5 minutes reframe to the goal. Next 50 minutes walk the full ACRR plan pillar by pillar, with reasoning. Next 25 minutes their reactions and questions. Final 10 minutes: get explicit, formal approval to begin the build, and confirm the Week 4 start. Building does not begin without this approval.

**What good looks like.** By end of Week 3 you have a complete, specific ACRR plan and the client has formally approved it. The client understands what is about to be built and has agreed to it. There are no open strategic questions heading into the build.

**Decision points and risks.** Do not let the build start without formal approval. Building from an unapproved plan is how you end up rebuilding work in Week 8 because the client "thought it would be different." The approval call is a real gate. Also: the audit and strategy work is the same quality bar as the Strategy package. The compression to three weeks is about pace, not depth. A shallow audit produces a build that solves the wrong problems.

## Phase 2 · Implementation (Weeks 4 to 9, done-for-you build)

**Purpose.** Build the entire patient growth system. This is the core of the Accelerator and the reason the client paid for it.

**The rhythm.** There is a live call every single week of this phase, 30 to 45 minutes each, six total. The rhythm is: you build during the week, you show the work on the weekly call using Loom plus live screen share, the client gives feedback and approves, you proceed. Momentum and visible progress are the point. The client should never wonder what is happening.

**The work, by sub-phase.**

*Attract (Weeks 4 to 5).*
- Improve or fully rebuild the website so it converts. Custom, written and designed around the ideal patient.
- Fully optimize the Google Business Profile against the eight-field standard.
- Create one strong lead magnet plus its landing page.
- Do the basic on-page SEO work on the website.
- Set up basic ad tracking.

*Convert (Weeks 6 to 7).*
- Set up an easy, high-converting booking system.
- Create intake forms that qualify leads. The forms are public-facing marketing intake, not clinical intake. They live in the client's own compliant tools.
- Build automated email and text follow-up sequences.
- Improve the consultation pages.

*Retain (Week 8).*
- Build a new patient welcome sequence.
- Create educational email sequences that keep patients engaged.
- Build re-engagement messaging for patients who go quiet.

*Refer (Weeks 8 to 9).*
- Set up automated referral requests.
- Create a testimonial collection system.
- Build refer-a-friend automation.

**The live calls.** Six weekly progress calls, one per week, 30 to 45 minutes. Each follows the same shape: quick recap of what you built this week, screen-share walkthrough, collect feedback and approvals, preview next week. Keep them tight. The call exists to maintain momentum and approval flow, not to redo strategy.

**What good looks like.** Each sub-phase ends with that part of the system built, reviewed, and approved by the client. The client has seen every piece as it was built and has approved designs and copy along the way, so nothing is a surprise at launch. The build stays on the 6-week schedule.

**Decision points and risks.** The biggest risk in this phase is approval drift: building a week ahead while last week's work is still unapproved, then having to unwind it. Do not get ahead of approvals. The weekly call is the control mechanism, so protect it and do not let it slip. Second risk: scope expansion. The client, seeing the work, asks for extra things. Some are small and fine. Anything that materially expands the build is a change order or a post-launch retainer item, named warmly and clearly, not absorbed silently. Third risk, the HIPAA line: everything you build is public-facing marketing or sits in the client's own compliant tools. You do not build anything that puts you in the path of patient data.

## Phase 3 · Launch and Handover (Weeks 10 to 12)

**Purpose.** Test everything, launch the system, train the client and their team, and close the project cleanly.

**The work.**
- Test the entire system thoroughly. Every form, every automation, every sequence, every booking path. Test as a patient would experience it.
- Make final fixes and improvements based on testing.
- Create simple documentation the client can keep.
- Run Training Session 1 (see below).
- Run the final review and project closeout call (see below).
- Deliver the performance report showing where things stand at launch.

**The live calls.**

*Week 10 or 11, Training Session 1 (60 minutes).* Train the client and their team on how to use the new systems. How the booking flows work, how the automations run, what they need to do and what runs itself. This is hands-on. The goal is that the team is genuinely comfortable, not just shown.

*Week 12, Final Review and Project Closeout Call (45 to 60 minutes).* Review the final results and where the system stands. Celebrate the wins honestly. Hand over the documentation. Then move naturally into the retainer conversation. After 12 weeks of delivering real work, the retainer offer is earned and credible. It is still an offer, not a push.

**What good looks like.** Every part of the system has been tested and works. The client and their team can use the system without you. The documentation is clear. The client has a performance report. The project ends on a definite, clean note, and the client knows their options for ongoing support.

**Decision points and risks.** Do not skip thorough testing to hit the Week 12 date. A system that launches broken undoes 11 weeks of good work and damages the trust that earns the retainer. If testing reveals real problems, it is better to communicate a short, honest delay than to launch something that fails in the client's hands. The closeout call should feel like a real ending: the project is complete, the work is handed over, and continued work is available if they want it.

# Standards that apply to both playbooks

**The eight-field Google Business Profile standard.** Whenever a playbook says "review" or "optimize" the Google Business Profile, this is the standard: categories (primary and secondary, correct not approximate), hours (including holiday hours, current), services (each listed individually with its own description), FAQ (real questions, real answers), photos (recent, real, not stock), reviews and review responses (responding to all of them), posts (active, recent), attributes (every applicable one toggled on).

**What good looks like, in general.** Every deliverable is specific to the practice it was built for. If a deliverable could be handed to a different practice without changes, it is not done. Specificity is the standard.

**The executability test.** Any plan or system handed to the client has to be usable by a time-poor physician who sees 6 to 14 patients a day. Impressive but unusable is a failure. Test every deliverable against the question: can a busy physician actually use this.

**The HIPAA boundary.** Public-facing marketing assets only. No PHI, ever. Anything involving patient data lives in the client's own compliant tools. The contract states you are not a Business Associate. If a client tries to pull you across this line, stop and redirect.

**The scope line.** The Strategy package is a plan. The Accelerator is a build. When a Strategy client wants things built, that is the Accelerator or a retainer, named warmly. When an Accelerator client wants the build expanded, that is a change order or a post-launch retainer item, named clearly. Scope creep absorbed silently is unpaid work and a blurred offer.
