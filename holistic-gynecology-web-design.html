The Problem

Why holistic gynecology practices keep ending up with the wrong website.

There are two obvious templates for a gynecology website. Neither one fits.

The first is conventional OB/GYN web design. Stock photos of a pregnant woman on a white background. Hospital-system brand language. A page called “Our Team” with a row of headshots in white coats. This design language is built for affiliated practices with multiple providers and a billing department. It communicates institutional medicine. That’s not wrong for the practices it’s built for. It’s wrong for a holistic or integrative gynecologist whose entire point of difference is that she is specifically not that.

The second template is wellness web design. Soft pink and sage. Watercolor botanical illustrations. Language that sounds more like a health coach than a credentialed clinician. This design language is built for practitioners without a medical license who need visual warmth to establish trust in the absence of credentials. A gynecologist with an MD or a CNM with fifteen years of clinical experience doesn’t need that scaffolding. She needs the design to reflect the depth of training she actually has.

The result: holistic gynecology practices end up with sites that look like a maternity ward, a yoga studio, or an awkward hybrid of both. None of them match the practice being run.

The patient who finds a holistic gynecology practice through search has usually been looking for a while. She wants integrative care and she wants it from someone with real clinical training. She can read a site in thirty seconds and know whether the practice understands what she’s looking for. The site that fails to signal both competence and philosophy loses her before she reaches the contact page. Holistic gynecology website design sits between two established templates, and neither one gets the balance right. The Build

What a holistic gynecology practice website actually needs.

Every Simple Rabbit build starts with copy. Before design decisions are made, I write what each page says. For a holistic gynecology practice, that process begins with understanding how this particular practitioner describes her work, which clinical offerings define the practice, and who her patient actually is.

The design sits between two poles on purpose: not pink-and-pastel, not stock pregnancy photos, not corporate-medical, not crystals-and-moon-phases. Clinically serious without reading as a hospital. Warm without reading as a life coach. The visual language earns trust with the patient who wants both.

The copy process is different for practitioners in this niche because the practitioner usually has strong, specific opinions about how she describes her work. Integrative gynecology language is precise. The difference between “holistic approach” and “evidence-informed integrative care” matters to her and to her patient. The writing process treats her language as the source material, not as something to be smoothed into generic wellness copy.

Service pages are built around the clinical offerings of a holistic gynecology practice, which look different from a conventional OB/GYN service list. That might include: an integrative annual exam, perimenopause and menopause care that isn’t organized around HRT-as-default, a functional approach to PCOS or endometriosis, sexual health, pelvic pain, or fertility support outside the IVF-only model. Each of those is its own page, written for the patient searching for that specific thing.

Booking and conversion paths are built to reflect the actual intake process. Holistic gynecology practices often use a longer intake call or pre-screening before the first appointment. That process should be explained on the site, not hidden. Patients who understand what to expect before they book are the ones who show up ready.

Common Questions

Common questions from holistic gynecology practitioners.

How is a holistic gynecology website different from a conventional OB/GYN website?

A conventional OB/GYN site is built for the patient who is looking for standard prenatal care or a routine annual exam, often within a hospital-affiliated system. A holistic gynecology website is built for the patient who has already decided she wants something different and is trying to assess whether this practice is the right fit. That patient reads differently. She is comparing philosophy, not just location and insurance acceptance. The copy needs to be specific to your clinical approach, the design needs to signal depth without reading as institutional, and the service pages need to name the conditions and models of care she is searching for specifically.

Do you work with midwives running gynecology-focused practices, not just birth-focused ones?

Yes. The CNM segment that focuses on ongoing women’s gynecologic health, annual exams, menopause, hormones, pelvic health, sexual health, rather than birth and obstetric care is distinct, and most web design for midwives defaults to the birth-focused template. A gynecology-focused midwifery website needs to make the scope of care clear from the first page, so the patient who isn’t looking for birth support understands immediately that this practice is for her. That requires specific copy and specific service page architecture that a generalist midwifery template won’t have.

How do you handle the language around topics like informed consent, BHRT, or alternatives to standard care?

Carefully, and with the practitioner as the authority. Holistic gynecologists often have strong, specific positions on how they discuss bioidentical hormones, informed consent, off-label treatments, and alternatives to standard protocols. My job is not to soften those positions to make the site more generic. The copy is written to reflect how you actually describe your work, reviewed by you before anything goes live, and structured so that the patient who is looking for that specific approach can identify herself as the right fit quickly. I don’t write medical content without your input and review.

Can you handle local SEO for “holistic gynecologist [my city]” or “integrative women’s health [my city]”?

Local SEO is the work that puts your practice in front of patients in your area when they search on Google. Every Simple Rabbit build includes the foundations: location signals on every relevant page, title tags built around real patient search terms, and a Google Business Profile that aligns with what the site says. Holistic gynecology and integrative women’s health are lower-volume search categories than conventional OB/GYN, which actually means less competition in many markets. The practices ranking for those terms are often doing so with a well-structured site and a current Google Business Profile, not an expensive SEO retainer.

My practice spans holistic gynecology, hormone care, and functional medicine. Which page should I be reading?

All of them are relevant, and that’s actually common. Many practitioners in this space sit across multiple niches. The page that matters most is the one that most closely describes the patient you most want to reach, which is also the framing the site is built around. If your practice is primarily gynecology-focused with a functional lens, this page describes the right approach. If hormone and menopause care is the center of what you do, the menopause practice page is closer. If root-cause investigation and lab work are central, the functional medicine page applies.
