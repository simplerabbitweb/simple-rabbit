Does the Simple Rabbit visual identity match the new niche?

simplerabbit.studio  ·  April 25, 2026  ·  5 advisors + peer review

The Verdict in One Line

The branding is one buyer behind the positioning. The verbal identity now says "studio for cash-pay functional medicine and women's health practices." The visual identity still says "personal brand for creative service providers." Two specific elements (the cursive wordmark and the founder photo) are doing most of the damage and both are fixable inside 30 days.

Element-by-Element Verdict

Cursive Wordmark
Replace Reads as wedding planner / stationery brand / wellness coach. The single biggest mismatch with the new niche.
Display Serif Headlines
Keep Editorial, confident, premium. The strongest visual asset on the site. Anchor the rest of the system around this.
Founder Photo
Replace Moto jacket, styled living room, decorative ladder. Reads "personal brand photographer 2021." Damaging because of size and prominence.
Peach Gradient
Refresh Pretty but too feminine-creative on its own. Keep as warmth, but discipline it with a clinical anchor color.
Off-White Base
Keep Editorial, calm, doesn't compete. Right foundation.
Solid Black Header
Refresh Reads as 2018-2020 boutique creative brand. A softer header (deep ink, dark forest, or clean light) would feel more current.
Article Photography
Replace Black-and-white stock of women in cafés reads "Substack newsletter." Article photos are doing brand work. Replace with medical-adjacent imagery, real client work, or strong type-only cards.
Monospace Eyebrows
Keep Adds a quietly editorial, slightly technical voice. Plays well against the display serif.

The Recommendation

Refresh aggressively, in three waves. Don't redesign from scratch.

The display serif, off-white base, and monospace eyebrows form a real foundation. The wordmark, founder photo, and stock article photography are the three pieces actively damaging the niche claim. Fix those three and the visual identity will be 80% of the way there.

Underlying frame: Decide whether Simple Rabbit is a personal brand (Leann who happens to do web design) or a studio brand (Simple Rabbit, run by Leann). Every visual decision flows from that. The new positioning needs studio-brand visuals.

This Week
Replace the cursive wordmark with a temporary lockup. Set "simple rabbit" or "Simple Rabbit" in the existing display serif (Canela/Mazius family). Lowercase, tight tracking, no flourish. This is a 2-hour change that solves 40% of the visual mismatch.
This Month
New founder photo. Different wardrobe (no moto jacket), different setting (clean studio, neutral wall, or a real workspace). Solid color top. Shoulders-up or normally seated. Cost: $300-$500 with a local photographer.
This Month
Replace article header images. Pull stock photos of women in cafés. Use medical-adjacent imagery, real client work where you have permission, or strong typography-only cards with a small color accent.
This Month
Add a clinical anchor color. One disciplined accent beyond the peach: deep ink blue, oxblood, or muted forest green. Use it on dividers, eyebrow tags, and small accents. Disciplines the whole palette.
Next 60 Days
Commission a real wordmark. From a designer who specializes in studio identity, not Fiverr. Budget: $1,500-$3,000. The temporary type lockup gets you most of the way there, but a custom wordmark is the long-term anchor.
Next 60 Days
Soften the black header. Test deep ink, very dark forest green, or a clean light header with a subtle bottom border. The current solid-black header is dating the site.

Blind Spots the Council Caught in Peer Review

1. The black header is dating the site. Solid-black headers were a 2018-2020 boutique creative brand signature. None of the advisors flagged it but it's now an aesthetic tell. Test alternatives.
2. The Romans 1:16 verse in the footer. A meaningful expression of faith that will resonate strongly with some buyers and create friction with others. In healthcare-adjacent positioning, it deserves a thoughtful brand decision rather than silent inclusion. This is yours to keep or move — flagging that it's doing brand work either way.
3. The "rabbit" name itself. Soft, gentle, feminine-coded. Works for the wellness-adjacent edge of the niche (women's health, holistic gynecology). Could read too soft for cash-pay psychiatry, concierge primary care, or longevity. Worth asking whether the name needs to lean harder into "Simple" and quieter into "Rabbit" through visual treatment.
4. Favicon and OG share image. The brand reaches buyers in Google search results, social shares, and link previews before they hit the site. Worth checking whether these align with the new positioning or still reflect the old.

Read Each Advisor in Full

The Contrarian — "The visuals are working against the niche. Hard."

The cursive wordmark is a wedding photographer's logo. The founder photo (moto jacket, styled couch, decorative ladder) is personal-brand-photographer 2021 — it signals "branding coach" or "Squarespace template seller," not "I have built websites for serious medical practices and understand HIPAA." The peach gradient is the third problem: the exact aesthetic of fashion blogs and yoga studios. The display serif is the strongest visual choice on the page — keep it, burn the rest down. The current visual identity is from the previous version of the business. It needs to be redone, not refreshed.

The First Principles Thinker — "Personal brand vs studio brand."

What should the visual identity actually communicate to a cash-pay functional medicine practice owner? Three things in priority: serious technical capability, niche fluency (medical, science-respecting, premium-but-not-luxury), and an accessible founder. The current visuals communicate those in reverse — leading with personal brand, barely signaling serious studio at all. Deeper question: is this Leann (personal brand) or Simple Rabbit (studio brand)? Current visuals scream personal brand. New positioning is starting to claim studio brand. Buyers feel that contradiction even if they can't articulate it.

The Expansionist — "There is no visual leader in this category yet."

Most cash-pay functional medicine and women's health websites currently default to corporate stock photos with sage green, navy-and-gold "concierge luxury," or day-spa wallpaper. Whoever builds the recognized aesthetic for cash-pay functional medicine — clinical without cold, warm without woo, premium without luxury — owns that category for a decade. Leann has the editorial display serif right (the foundation). Missing: a studio-mark wordmark (Pentagram or Mucca energy), a color system with a clinical anchor (ink, surgical green, oxblood), and photography that signals practice not personal brand. Done right, this becomes a competitive advantage the niche didn't know it was missing.

The Outsider — "The branding is one buyer behind the positioning."

Land on the hero. First thing: cursive logo. I think feminine creative business — calligrapher, stationery designer, wedding planner. Then I read the headline and feel whiplash. Scroll to the founder photo: she looks like every female founder on Instagram, not a person who builds websites for doctors. Articles page: women drinking coffee in black and white. Substack newsletter, not medical practice content. Net impression: thoughtful, premium-ish, feminine, creative. Right brand for a solo therapist, women's coach, stationery brand, or wedding photographer. Wrong brand for a website for a dermatology practice.

The Executor — "Don't redo everything. Redo these things in this order."

Week 1: Replace the cursive wordmark with a temporary type lockup using the existing display serif (2-hour project). Get a new founder photo — different setting, different wardrobe, no moto jacket ($300-$500 with a local photographer). Month 1: Replace article header images with medical-adjacent imagery or strong type-only cards. Add one clinical anchor color (deep ink, oxblood, or muted forest green). Month 2: Commission a proper wordmark from a studio-identity designer ($1,500-$3,000). By end of next week, the site reads "serious studio for healthcare practices" instead of "personal brand for service providers." Without redesigning the whole site.
