# Cheat Sheet: The 5 Buyer Signals

A practical guide for women's wellness practitioners on recognizing when a patient (or a client) is ready to invest, what they're actually saying, and how to respond.

---

## The 5 Signals

### Signal #1: They're DONE

**Meaning:** Exhausted from trying to figure it out alone. She's tried Squarespace, she's tried hiring a friend, she's tried the cheap freelancer on Upwork. She's ready to hand the whole thing to someone who knows what they're doing.

**Language she uses:**
- "I'm done piecing this together on my own."
- "I'm done trying to convince patients my work is worth what I charge."
- "If I have to redo my homepage one more time, I'll scream."

**Why it converts:** Emotional exhaustion is a clarifying force. Once she's done, the next decision happens fast. She's not comparison shopping. She's looking for someone she can trust to take it off her plate.

**Messaging tip:** Lead with relief. Don't sell the deliverable. Sell the end of the loop she's been stuck in.

> "If you're tired of redoing your website every six months, here's the version you don't have to redo."

---

### Signal #2: Urgency

**Meaning:** She has a real-world deadline. A launch date. A new specialty rolling out. A practice opening. A speaking engagement. A pivot from insurance to private-pay where the website needs to match the new positioning before the first patient calls. The clock is the buying trigger.

**Language she uses:**
- "I'm opening my practice in 90 days and I have no website yet."
- "I'm doing a podcast interview next month and my site is humiliating."
- "I'm transitioning to private-pay on January 1st and I'm not ready."

**Why it converts:** When timing is real, price becomes secondary. She'll pay more for someone who can actually hit the date than for someone cheaper who can't.

**Messaging tip:** Name the deadline. Make the timeline visible. Confirm you can hit it.

> "If you need to be live before your practice opens, here's exactly how the next 6 weeks would go."

---

### Signal #3: Pattern Recognition

**Meaning:** She's been around the block enough times to see the cycle. The wrong patients keep calling. The website keeps going stale. The bookings come in for the cheap services and not the premium ones. She knows it's not bad luck. It's the system she has, and she wants someone to break it.

**Language she uses:**
- "I keep attracting patients who balk at my pricing."
- "Every web designer I've worked with disappears after launch."
- "I've redesigned my homepage three times and it still doesn't bring in the right patients."

**Why it converts:** She's not asking for hope. She's asking for a different mechanism. Show her the mechanism, and the conversion is fast.

**Messaging tip:** Name the pattern back to her, then show the part of your offer that breaks it.

> "If the wrong patients keep finding you, the website is the filter. Here's how a custom build changes who books."

---

### Signal #4: Willingness to Invest in Quality

**Meaning:** She's already learned that cheap is expensive. She's bought the templates. She's tried the $500 freelancer. She knows now that the discount path is what got her into this mess. She's ready to pay for one-on-one work with someone who knows the niche.

**Language she uses:**
- "I don't want a template. I want something built for my practice specifically."
- "I want to work with the person who's actually doing the work."
- "I'm not the cheapest option for my patients. I don't need the cheapest option for myself."

**Why it converts:** Quality buyers prioritize the work over the line item. Once she sees the craftsmanship and the niche fit, price becomes a footnote.

**Messaging tip:** Emphasize the partnership. Name what's premium and why it matters.

> "You work with me directly. I write the copy, design the site, handle the SEO, and stay on as your partner. Nothing gets handed off."

---

### Signal #5: Willingness to Invest in Convenience

**Meaning:** She knows she could do it. She's smart enough, capable enough, has a Canva login. She just doesn't want to. Her time is worth more than the project, and she'd rather pay to make it disappear from her to-do list.

**Language she uses:**
- "I know I should be writing my own copy, but I don't have the headspace."
- "I just want someone to handle this so I can focus on patients."
- "I don't want to learn WordPress. I want it managed."

**Why it converts:** Convenience buyers are buying their time back. The faster and more handled the offer feels, the more they'll pay for it.

**Messaging tip:** Lead with the off-her-plate value. Don't make her hire a project manager. Don't make her learn anything. Don't make her "stay involved."

> "I'll write the copy. I'll build the site. I'll keep it running after launch. Your involvement is one questionnaire and a handful of approvals."

---

## How to Use This

When a discovery call comes in, listen for which signal she's giving. Most buyers give two. Match your messaging to the strongest one.

**A note on Signal #1 and #5:** They sound similar but they're not. "Done" is emotional exhaustion. "Convenience" is calm prioritization. The Done buyer wants relief. The Convenience buyer wants efficiency. Same offer, different pitch.

**A note on Signal #4:** This buyer is the most natural fit for the offer, but she's also the slowest to commit. She's evaluating. Show your work, name your process, and trust her to decide on her timeline.

**The signal that doesn't appear here:** Price-shopping. If a prospect leads with price, she's not in any of these five buckets. She's not ready, or she's not the right fit. Don't try to sell her into one of these signals. Let her go.
