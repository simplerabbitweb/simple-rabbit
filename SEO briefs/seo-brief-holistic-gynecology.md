# SEO Brief: Web Design for Holistic Gynecology Practices

## The buyer

A practitioner running a holistic, integrative, or midwifery-supportive gynecology practice. Most often an MD or DO who is board-certified in OB/GYN and additionally trained or certified in integrative or holistic medicine, sometimes a Certified Nurse Midwife (CNM) running an independent gynecology-focused practice, sometimes an NP with an OB/GYN clinical background. She is most often 40 to 60, often with 15 to 35 years of clinical history, and her decision to practice holistically is deliberate and articulate. She talks about her work in terms of philosophy as much as services. She uses phrases like "root cause," "informed consent," "bodily integrity," "with woman" (for the midwives), and "beyond birth control and antidepressants" to describe what she does.

This is the most professionally distinct buyer of the four pages. She is not a wellness coach who added gynecology to her offerings. She is a fully credentialed gynecological clinician who chose to practice differently from conventional OB/GYN. She is tired of being categorized as either a regular OB/GYN (which she's not, anymore) or a wellness practitioner (which underplays her clinical depth). The page has to recognize that and not flatten her into either category.

Her stage is most often C (established 2 to 20 years in independent practice) or B (recently transitioned out of a hospital system or large group OB/GYN practice). Stage A is rare in this niche because the transition from conventional gynecology to holistic gynecology takes years of additional training and reflection. By the time she's looking for a website designer, she's already deep into the practice she wants to build.

A note on the share of midwives: an unusual share of practitioners in this niche are CNMs (Certified Nurse Midwives) running gynecology-focused practices rather than birth-focused practices. The page should welcome this group explicitly. They're often overlooked by both conventional OB/GYN-focused designers and by general wellness designers, and they're an underserved segment of the pool.

## Primary target keyword

**"web design for holistic gynecology practice"** (and the closely related "holistic gynecology website" and "website for holistic gynecology").

Volume note: very low. This is the lowest-volume primary keyword across the four industry pages, and the brief should be honest about that. The phrase has fewer than 10 monthly searches in most data tools. The buyer pool is small (a few hundred to maybe a few thousand active independent holistic gynecology practices nationwide) and the search volume reflects that.

The honest read: this page is not chasing a high-volume keyword. It's chasing a specific buyer who arrives via a small handful of high-intent searches and via referrals from other parts of the Simple Rabbit content ecosystem. The page is worth building because the buyer who searches for this exact phrase has already filtered themselves into the perfect-fit category. There is no patient buyer typing "web design for holistic gynecology practice." There is only the practitioner. Conversion potential per visitor is unusually high, even though raw traffic will be low.

Settled vs. educated guess: It's settled that no competitor is currently targeting this phrase directly (every result on the SERP for variations of "holistic gynecology website" is either a patient-facing holistic gynecology practice site or a generic OB/GYN web design agency). It's an educated guess that the page can rank in the top 3 within 3 to 6 months because the SERP is empty of real competitors.

This is closer to the menopause page situation than the functional medicine or aesthetics situations. Wide open, low traffic, high intent.

The risk of cannibalization between the holistic gynecology page and the menopause page is real.

If the keyword is rooted in functional or integrative medicine as a discipline, the FM page owns it. The three pages link to each other to handle the practitioners who span multiple categories.

The overlap is a feature, not a bug, if it's managed deliberately. The interlinking ecosystem can route a holistic gyn who also does heavy hormone work between two pages, and Google can rank both for their respective queries without confusion.

## Page structure recommendation

Page length: 1,400 to 1,800 words. Shorter than the other three industry pages, in line with the lower search volume and tighter buyer pool. The page doesn't need 2,500 words to rank for queries with low volume. The buyer also reads carefully (she's a credentialed clinician), which means quality matters more than length. Less padding, more precision.

**H1:** Web design for holistic gynecology practices

(Keyword placement: primary phrase used naturally.)

**Hero subhead:** Custom websites for independent gynecologists, midwives, and integrative women's health practitioners. Built to look as serious as the work and to bring in patients who are looking for exactly what you do.

(Keyword placement: "holistic gynecologists" implied via the H1, "midwives" and "integrative women's health practitioners" both appear naturally. The "as serious as the work" phrase is doing real work for this buyer specifically, who is allergic to anything that looks unmedical.)

**Section 1, the problem:**

H2: Why holistic gynecology practices keep ending up with the wrong website

Body covers the specific bind this practitioner is in. Generic OB/GYN web design (the kind that ranks for "OB/GYN website design") is built for hospital-affiliated conventional practices, with stock photos of pregnant women and corporate hospital-system messaging. Generic wellness web design is built for coaches and health practitioners without a medical license, and reads as too soft and unmedical. Neither fits. The result: holistic gynecologists end up with sites that either look like a maternity ward or like a yoga studio, and neither one matches the practice they actually run.

This section can be 250 to 350 words. Naming the specific bind directly is the move that hooks this buyer. She has experienced exactly this and probably hasn't seen anyone describe it accurately on a web design site.

Keywords this section brushes against: "holistic gynecology website," "integrative gynecology website design," "midwifery website."

**Section 2, the offer:**

H2: What a holistic gynecology practice website actually needs

Body covers what a Simple Rabbit build for this niche includes, with the specific elements named:

- Custom design that reads as clinically serious without reading as a hospital. Mention specifically: not pink-and-pastel, not stock pregnancy photos, not corporate-medical, not crystals-and-moon-phases either. Something between the two.
- Strategic copywriting that respects clinical depth. Mention that the practitioner usually has strong opinions about how she describes her work and that the writing process treats her language as the source material, not as something to be smoothed out.
- Local SEO foundations (define the term: showing up in local Google searches when patients in your area search for "holistic gynecologist," "integrative women's health," "midwife gynecologist" plus your city.)
- Service pages built around the specific clinical offerings of a holistic gynecology practice, which look different from conventional OB/GYN service pages. Mention examples without overpromising scope: integrative annual exam, perimenopause and menopause care without HRT-as-default, functional approach to PCOS or endometriosis, sexual health, pelvic pain, fertility outside the IVF mill model.
- Booking and conversion paths that match the practice's actual intake process (these practices often have longer intake calls or pre-screening before the first appointment, which should be reflected on the site).
- Ongoing care plan
- One point of contact (you, Leann)

Length: 350 to 450 words.

Keywords: "holistic gynecology website," "integrative gynecology website design," "midwife website design," "women's health practice website."

**Section 3, the proof:**

H2: A note on proof

This is the section that handles the proof gap differently than the menopause and FM pages. Two things are true at once: there's no published case study in this niche, AND the buyer here is comparing Simple Rabbit against generalist designers who don't understand her work, not against five other niche-aware competitors. The proof gap matters less because the alternative she's evaluating is worse.

The brief recommends a short, honest section (150 to 200 words) that names the situation directly. Something like: "I haven't published a case study in holistic gynecology yet. What I can tell you: I work in adjacent women's wellness niches, I've audited dozens of holistic and integrative practice sites, I know the patterns that hold practices back, and I'm building a small portfolio of holistic gynecology work this year." Reference the Smooth Laser Center case study briefly as adjacent women's wellness proof. Link to it. Don't oversell.

This section is shorter than the other pages because the buyer doesn't need a long proof section. She needs honesty and competence. Padding it out with adjacent case studies and audit pattern recognition would feel performative to this specifically-articulate buyer.

**Section 4, the process:**

H2: How a holistic gynecology website project actually works

Reuse the three-step process from the Services page. Adapt the language: mention the longer discovery conversation this practitioner often needs (because her positioning is more specific than most), the copy process that treats her clinical language as source material, the support for booking flows that match longer intake processes.

Keep this section tight: 200 to 250 words. The process content is already explained on the Services page; this version just signals niche awareness.

**Section 5, the FAQ:**

H2: Common questions from holistic gynecology practitioners

This section captures the most long-tail SEO weight on the page. Five questions, each one a real search.

1. **How is a holistic gynecology website different from a conventional OB/GYN website?** (High-intent buyer query, low competition.)
2. **Do you work with midwives running gynecology-focused practices, not just birth-focused ones?** (Captures the CNM segment that other designers miss.)
3. **How do you handle the language around topics like informed consent, BHRT, or alternatives to standard care?** (Captures the buyer's concern about being smoothed out by a designer who doesn't understand her positioning.)
4. **Can you handle the SEO for "[my city] holistic gynecologist" or "integrative women's health [my city]"?** (Local SEO query, define the term in context.)
5. **What if my practice spans holistic gynecology, hormone care, AND functional medicine?** (Captures the practitioner who fits multiple Simple Rabbit industry pages, and routes her appropriately. This is the cross-page interlinking move done as an FAQ answer.)

Each answer 90 to 120 words. This section runs 500 to 600 words and carries most of the page's long-tail SEO weight.

**Section 6, the CTA:**

H2: Ready when you are

Standard sitewide CTA block. Soft secondary CTA to the playbook signup. This buyer often spends time researching before reaching out, so the playbook is a reasonable on-ramp.

## Competitive notes

Top ranking pages for variations of "holistic gynecology website design" and where the openings are:

The honest finding from the research: there are no real competitors targeting this niche specifically. The SERP for "holistic gynecology website design" and adjacent queries surfaces:

**1. Generic OB/GYN web design agencies** (Officite, GMR Web Team, WebToMed, Glacial, MyAdvice, PracticeBeat, O360)
All target conventional OB/GYN. None of them speak to the holistic, integrative, or midwifery-supportive practitioner. The buyer for this page would scroll past every one of them.
The opening: there's no opening to fight for, there's only an empty SERP slot to claim.

**2. General women's wellness designers**
A few designers exist in adjacent women's wellness (the same designers who showed up in the other three briefs: Mila Design Co for med spas, Angelique Vestil for functional medicine). None of them target holistic gynecology specifically.
The opening: the page can claim the niche cleanly because it's currently uncategorized in the design world.

**3. Patient-facing holistic gynecology practice sites** (Holistic Gynecology New York, TaraMD, Visionary Women's Health, Cohen Medical Practice, Functional Midwifery, Woman Wise Integrative Gynecology)
These aren't competitors. They're the buyer pool. They show up in the SERP because their sites use the phrase "holistic gynecology" heavily, but they're patient-facing practices, not designers. Studying these sites is useful (they show what a good holistic gynecology site can look like, and what the language patterns of the niche are), but they're not competitors for ranking on practitioner-side searches.

**The clean read on the competitive landscape:** this is the industry page with the least competition of the four. The trade-off is the lowest search volume. The page is an "own the niche" play, not a "fight for the spot" play.

## Open questions

These need your input before the page is built.

**1. The midwife angle.**
The brief recommends explicitly welcoming Certified Nurse Midwives running gynecology-focused practices. They're an underserved subgroup with real practices and real budget, and they search using slightly different language ("midwifery website design," "midwife gynecology website"). Want to confirm that scope, or do you want the page to focus narrower on physician-led holistic gynecology only? My recommendation: include the midwives. They're a clean fit for the studio's offer and the SERP currently underserves them.

**2. The proof section approach.**
The brief recommends a shorter, more honest proof section than the menopause and FM pages, because the buyer here is comparing against generalist designers (not niche-aware competitors). Want to confirm that direction, or keep proof structurally consistent with the other pages? My take: shorter and more direct. The buyer respects honesty and bounces fast on padding.

**3. Page length.**
The brief recommends 1,400 to 1,800 words, shorter than the other three industry pages. Two reasons: lower search volume doesn't reward depth-for-depth's-sake, and the buyer reads carefully and prefers precision over thoroughness. Want to confirm that length, or scale up to match the other pages? My take: keep it tight. Length for its own sake is a tax on this specific buyer's reading time.

**4. Pricing.**
Same question as the previous three pages. Holistic gynecology builds usually fall in the middle of the four niches in terms of scope (more service pages than aesthetics, fewer integrations than functional medicine). If the build is $7K-$12K, the page should feel premium and considered. If it's $5K-$9K, the page can lean more accessible. My read: this buyer is more comfortable with a higher price tag than the menopause buyer because she's been a practicing physician longer and has higher built-in revenue.

**5. The "with woman" language for midwives.**
The phrase "with woman" comes up repeatedly in midwifery positioning (it's the literal etymology of the word "midwife"). Including this phrase somewhere on the page, probably in Section 2 when describing the midwife sub-segment, would land hard with that buyer. Should the page lean into that language, or stay broader and let the midwives self-select via Question 2 in the FAQ? My take: include it once, in passing, so the midwife who reads it knows you know.
