# SEO Brief: Web Design for Women's Hormone and Menopause Practices

## The buyer

A female practitioner (NP, MD, DO, PA, or naturopath) who runs or is launching a private-pay practice focused on perimenopause, menopause, and hormone care. She is most often 35 to 55, science-trained, and somewhere between considering leaving insurance and 5 years off it. She knows menopause care is having a cultural moment, but the practice is hers alone, and her current website was either built on Squarespace, by a friend, or by an agency that treated her like a generic medical client. She is searching for someone who understands her work, her patient, and what makes a practice in this specific niche actually findable on Google.

## Primary target keyword

**"web design for menopause practice"** (and the closely related "website design for menopause practice").

Volume note: low to very low based on what's currently surfacing. Nobody is targeting it directly, which is the opportunity. This is a "be the first quality result" play, not a "fight for an existing pie" play. The page needs to anchor on this phrase and the broader cluster around it (see secondary keywords) so it captures intent across volume levels.

Settled vs. educated guess: It's settled that no one is currently dominating this exact phrase, the SERP is a mix of Dribbble portfolios, patient-facing menopause sites, and one-off design case studies. It's an educated guess that the phrase will pull qualified traffic, the page should be built around the cluster, not just the phrase, so it ranks for the searches that are actually being run.

## Page structure recommendation

**H1:** Web design for women's hormone and menopause practices.

(Plain language, primary keyword in front, no marketing flourish. Reads like a service description.)

**Hero subhead:** Custom websites and ongoing care for hormone and menopause specialists who want the right patients to find them.

(Picks up "hormone and menopause specialists" as a secondary signal. "Right patients to find them" matches the buyer's stated frustration with wrong-fit patients.)

**Section 1 (problem) — H2:** Why most menopause practice websites don't bring in the right patient.

Body covers two problem queries directly: the site looks like every other women's wellness practice in the area, and the site doesn't show up when patients in her town search for "menopause specialist" or "perimenopause doctor near me." 200 to 300 words. Mention "local search," "Google Business Profile," and "menopause specialist" naturally.

**Section 2 (offer) — H2:** What goes into a website built for a menopause practice.

Body covers what the build includes. Use this section to land the service-specific keywords without listing them. Topics: custom design (no templates), copy written for the perimenopause and menopause patient, local SEO foundations, Google Business Profile alignment, conversion paths for booking and consult inquiries, ongoing care after launch. 300 to 400 words.

**Section 3 (proof) — H2:** Built by someone who knows the niche.

This is the proof gap section. With no published case study yet in this exact niche, the section leans on three things: (a) Smooth Laser Center as adjacent women's wellness proof of how a custom site changed a practice's visibility, (b) audit pattern recognition (you've seen what's not working on dozens of menopause practice sites), (c) Leann's named perspective on why generic medical sites don't work for this niche. 200 to 250 words. Do not over-promise rankings. Do not invent a case study.

**Section 4 (process) — H2:** From first call to launched menopause practice site.

Reuses the general 3-step process from the Services page, with a few sentences of niche-specific context. Targets long-tail like "menopause practice website project" and "menopause web design timeline." 200 to 300 words.

**Section 5 (FAQ) — H2:** Common questions from menopause and hormone practice owners.

Each question is a real long-tail search. Suggested set:

- How is a website for a menopause practice different from a regular medical website?
- Will my menopause practice site rank for "menopause specialist near me"?
- I'm transitioning from OB/GYN to menopause-focused care. Can the website handle the rebrand?
- Do I need a separate page for perimenopause and menopause?
- Can the site handle a Menopause Society Certified Practitioner (MSCP) badge and credentials clearly?
- I do telehealth across multiple states. How does that affect the website's local SEO?

300 to 500 words across all answers. Each answer 50 to 80 words. Don't pad.

**Section 6 (CTA) — H2:** Tell me about your menopause practice.

Conversion-focused. One paragraph, one button, one form. The buyer is busy. Don't make her hunt.

**Total word count:** 1,500 to 2,000 words.

## Competitive notes

There are no direct competitors targeting "web design for menopause practice." Three adjacent players to be aware of:

**1. The Real Social Company (therealsocialcompany.com)**
What they do well: clean positioning around "hormone clinic digital marketing," named services, fast-loading site. They cover web design, SEO, and social.
The opening: they treat hormone clinics as a single category, heavy on TRT and HRT for men. The female practitioner running a perimenopause and menopause practice is not who their copy speaks to. Simple Rabbit can take the women-led, female-patient corner of this market without competing on their TRT-focused turf.

**2. Wellness Clinic Marketing (wellnessclinicmarketing.com)**
What they do well: long, keyword-stuffed pages that target a wide hormone marketing cluster. They have decent SERP presence for "hormone clinic marketing."
The opening: their copy is generic and reads agency-template. Phrases like "high-ticket wellness patients" and "high-converting Google Ads campaigns" are exactly the language Leann's buyer is allergic to. Simple Rabbit's voice (calm, direct, first-person, niche-specific) is the differentiator. Don't compete on volume, compete on fit.

**3. Functional Medicine SEO (functionalmedicineseo.com)**
What they do well: niche positioning, named services, focused on the functional medicine cluster (which includes hormone work).
The opening: they're SEO-only, not web design. They're a service provider Leann's buyer might already be using and frustrated by, because SEO without a site rebuild rarely fixes the underlying problem. Simple Rabbit is the one-stop alternative: site + SEO + ongoing care.

The bigger picture: the SERP for "web design for menopause practice" is currently held by Dribbble portfolios, design agencies that did one menopause-themed brand once, and patient-facing menopause sites. None of those are a real competitor for an industry-specific service page. This is a clean opening.

This builds topical authority across the niche and gives Google a clear map of the industry-specific service architecture.

## Open questions

These need your input before the page gets built.

**1. The proof gap.**
You flagged it. The brief works around it with adjacent proof and audit pattern recognition. Are you comfortable with that approach, or would you rather hold the page in draft until you have a published case study in this exact niche? My take: ship it with the workaround. The page will start building search authority on day one, and a case study can drop into Section 3 the moment you have one. Holding the page costs you 4 to 6 months of ranking time and gains you nothing.

**2. How specific should the page get on subspecialties within the niche?**
The buyer pool here splits into a few groups: dedicated menopause specialists (often MSCP-certified), OB/GYNs who do menopause, functional medicine practitioners with a hormone focus, and BHRT-focused practices. The page can speak to all of them under the "women's hormone and menopause" umbrella, or it can lean harder into one of those subgroups. My recommendation is to keep the umbrella broad and use the FAQ and supporting blog content to catch the subspecialty searches. That said, if your sales pipeline is currently weighted toward one of those subgroups, tell me and I'll narrow the page accordingly.

**3. What's the current pricing positioning for this niche?**
Not to put pricing on the page, but to inform the tone. If the build for this niche is $8K and up, the page should feel premium and unhurried. If it's $4K and up, the page can lean a little more accessible. Either works, but they read differently.

**4. Is there an MSCP angle worth playing?**
The Menopause Society Certified Practitioner credential is the gold standard in the niche. If you have any connection to the certified practitioner community (a current client, a referral source, a content collaborator), naming it on the page is high-leverage proof. If not, leave it. Don't fake credibility you don't have.
