01   Studio

About Simple Rabbit

Simple Rabbit is a one-person web design studio run by Leann Frank out of Bergen County, NJ. The studio builds custom WordPress websites for private-pay women's wellness practices: functional medicine, women's health, concierge. Everything is built one at a time.

01

The Independent Provider

NP, MD, DO, or PA who left insurance-based practice. Often a woman, 35–55, running functional medicine, women's health, or concierge. Smart, science-trained, skeptical of marketing.

02

The Growing Practice

2–5 years in, ready to raise prices, knows the website is holding them back. Looking for a studio that understands their world without needing a primer on private-pay medicine.

03

The Researcher

Not ready yet. Will spend 20 minutes on the site. The voice must hold up under scrutiny. Doesn't respond to pressure or generic agency language.

04   Voice

Brand Voice

Three qualities. All three at once is the target. Missing any one breaks it.

Direct.

Fewest words that work. No padding, no hedging, no overselling. If a sentence can be cut in half, cut it.

Warm.

Treat the reader as a peer, not a prospect. Contractions. Sounds like a real person talking to another real person.

Quietly confident.

No shouting. No credential-listing. Specificity does the credibility work. Let the work speak.

Direct without warm = cold.   Warm without direct = soft.   Confident without warm = arrogant.
05   Tone

Tone by Format

The voice stays constant. The register shifts depending on context.

Format Register
Homepage / Services Most polished. Short, confident statements. Every word earns its place.
Articles / Newsletter More conversational. First person, "you," occasional asides. Like Leann talking with a smart friend who runs a practice.
Client emails / Proposals Warmest. First person, signed Leann (first name only). Direct but human.
Social (IG, LinkedIn) Same warmth as articles, shorter. One idea per post. No hooks, no emoji lists.
Case Studies Most specific. Names, places, numbers. Client words and data carry the credibility.
06   Writing Rules

Sentence Rules

01 Short sentences over long. Vary length, lean short.
02 Active voice. "We build custom sites," not "Custom sites are built."
03 Specific nouns. "A menopause specialist," not "a healthcare provider."
04 Concrete verbs. "Brings in patients," not "drives engagement." "Ranks on Google," not "achieves visibility."
05 Cut qualifying clauses: "in order to," "as well as," "in terms of."
06 Use "I" when it's more honest. Leann is the studio. "I built this site," not "we built this site."
07 No false urgency. No "limited spots," "booking out fast," or "only 3 slots left."
08 No medical claims. No specific patient outcome claims from a website. No medical advice in any context.
07   Patterns to Avoid

Sentence Structures to Skip

These patterns signal AI-generated copy. Cut them on sight.

✕ "Not just X, but Y" — don't use it.
✕ "We don't just X, we Y" — don't use it.
✕ Triple adjective lists: "Clean, intentional, and strategic." One is sharper.
✕ "But here's the thing." Just say the thing.
✕ Em-dashes. Hard rule: never. Use a comma, period, parentheses, or colon instead.
✕ "And here's why." / "Because here's the truth." Just make the point.
✕ "Imagine if..." opener — skip it.

09   Quality Check

Three-Question Gut Check

Run any piece of copy through these before it goes live.

1

Would a real person say this out loud to another real person?

2

Could this same sentence appear on any web design agency's site? If yes, it's too generic.

3

Does it respect the reader's time and intelligence?
